Research behind Citius

Two IBM Research papers are the basis of Citius. The first paper measures how difficult an algorithm change is with current cryptographic APIs. The second paper designs the API that Citius implements.

An Assessment Framework for Application-Level Cryptographic Agility

Navaneeth Rameshan and Gregoire Messmer, IBM Research Europe, Zurich. arXiv:2606.13425, cs.CR, 11 June 2026.

We introduce a component-based assessment framework that characterizes application-level cryptographic agility along seven orthogonal dimensions: three coupling dimensions that measure what the application code knows about algorithms and providers, a cross-cutting decoupling mechanism, a governance authority dimension, and two agility enablers that measure actual migration capability.

Intent-Based Cryptographic API Design for Cryptographic Agility

Navaneeth Rameshan and Gregoire Messmer, IBM Research Europe, Zurich. arXiv:2606.13445, cs.CR, 11 June 2026.

As organizations move toward post-quantum cryptography, they face the major challenge of updating cryptographic algorithms across large, complex software portfolios. However, most cryptographic APIs in use today were designed around specific algorithms. … With this approach, updating cryptography becomes an operational process without the need to rewrite application code.

Read the design paperDownload the PDF

Contributions of the paper

  • Thirteen design principles, from five properties: abstraction, stability, temporal flexibility, separation and extensibility.
  • A scope vocabulary that names intent. Key creation does not name an algorithm.
  • Two separate registries: one for templates and one for providers.
  • A policy abstraction with two-phase evaluation. It does not set a policy format.
  • Three types of key evolution: rotation, transformation and migration. Each key keeps its version history.

The paper ends with a full migration scenario. The post-quantum transition is an operational procedure, and the application code does not change.
